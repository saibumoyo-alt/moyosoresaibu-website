7 retention levers

Where are you losing trust?

Start with the leak. Then fix the system around it.

01

Your customer had a problem.

Fix it fast.

A problem does not have to end the relationship. A weak recovery often does.

  • Acknowledge the issue.
  • Own the next step.
  • Close the loop.
Research note: a 2025 peer-reviewed study found satisfactory service recovery can rebuild engagement and loyalty.
02

Your customer feels ignored.

Make them feel seen.

People stay when the service feels human, not scripted.

  • Use their context.
  • Listen before replying.
  • Give staff room to help.
2025 signal: 73% in Zurich's global study said they avoid businesses that lack empathy.
03

Your customer repeats the same story.

Remove the friction.

WhatsApp, email, social and support should feel like one conversation.

  • Keep customer context.
  • Share notes across channels.
  • Do not restart the problem.
Salesforce reports 56% often have to repeat information to different representatives.
04

Your experience feels generic.

Make it relevant.

Useful personalization can increase repeat buying. Bad personalization destroys trust.

  • Use real preferences.
  • Recommend with a reason.
  • Explain how data is used.
McKinsey found 78% said personalized content made them more likely to repurchase.
05

Your customer meant to stay.

Stop accidental churn.

Failed cards and missed payments should not silently cancel a good relationship.

  • Retry smartly.
  • Warn before expiry.
  • Make payment updates easy.
Stripe says nearly a quarter of churn is involuntary and Smart Retries recover 57% of recurring payments that originally failed on average.
06

Your loyal customer gets nothing extra.

Reward staying.

The reward should be easy to understand and worth using.

  • Keep the value simple.
  • Reward useful behaviour.
  • Make redemption easy.
Deloitte's 2025 survey found 72% say loyalty programs make them more likely to spend with a preferred brand.
07

Your customer talks in public.

Show up there.

Social service is part of the customer experience, not only marketing.

  • Respond quickly.
  • Move sensitive issues private.
  • Keep the public follow-up visible.
McKinsey reports customers spend 20–40% more with companies that respond to service requests on social media.
